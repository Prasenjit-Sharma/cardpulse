How the colour is chosen

One accent, three proportionsAbout 60% paper or dark ground, 30% ink, 10% accent. A card with two loud colours reads as a flyer.
Match the paper to the accentCool accents (navy, teal, indigo) sit on cool-white paper; warm ones (copper, burgundy) look richer on warm paper. Mixing temperatures reads as a mistake.
Contrast first, taste secondEvery accent is at least 5.7:1 against paper, and carries white text at 5.9:1 or better. Small text on a card is read on a phone in a bright hall.
The QR is never colouredDark modules on a white plate with a clear margin, on every design. A tinted QR fails in poor light, and the QR is the point of the card.
Dark cards use a lighter twinA dark accent on a dark card disappears. Each accent has a lighter, slightly softer twin (7.2:1 or better on the dark ground) used only there.

The six designs

All share the same proportions as a real business card (3.5 by 2 in). Each puts the name first, then the way to reach the person, with the QR always in a fixed, quiet spot.

My recommendation

Ship five, keep one in reserve.
  1. Ledger as the default. It is the CardPulse look: quiet, exact, and reads well small.
  2. Header for people who want colour at first glance.
  3. Split when there is a logo or photo, since the panel gives it a proper place.
  4. Noir for consultants and founders; it stands out in a stack of white cards.
  5. Bold for stalls: the name reads from across a table.
  6. Tint is the friendliest but least formal. Keep it back unless you want a sixth.
